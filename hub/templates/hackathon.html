What makes a good challenge?

  • A real need. A problem a clinician, patient group or research team actually has.
  • Data that can be used. Open, synthetic or already-approved data that teams can work with during the hackathon. Never upload patient data to the hub.
  • The right size. Something a small team can make visible progress on in a few days.
  • A challenge owner. Someone who can answer questions on the day and judge what "useful" looks like.

How submission works

  1. Create your profile and agree to the Code of Conduct.
  2. Propose a project with the type Hackathon, including how collaborators will be credited (authorship, acknowledgements, code release and publication approval).
  3. Your challenge stays on hold while the committee checks it against the panel criteria: clinical impact, data readiness, resources and AI feasibility. They may ask you for changes, and you'll be emailed their decision.
  4. Once approved, your challenge is published below and participants can ask to join.

Who can take part?

Clinicians, researchers, students, engineers and data scientists at King's. You don't need to be an AI expert: clinical insight is just as valuable.
