Code of Conduct

How we work together on the FoLSM AI Collaboration Hub. The core idea is simple: agree the important things before you start, and nothing leaves the project without both parties' approval.

{% endblock %} {% block body %}
In short
  1. Agree IP, authorship, data use and publication terms with your collaborators a priori: before any work begins.
  2. Publications, presentations, code releases and any other outcomes need the written approval of both parties.
  3. What each party brings (data, code, models, know-how) stays theirs unless you agree otherwise.
  4. Respect patient data, information governance and each other.

1. Who this applies to

Everyone with a FoLSM AI Collaboration Hub account: proposers, Principal Investigators (PIs), collaborators, hackathon participants, reviewers and panel members.

Most projects bring together two parties:

  • The proposing party: the person or group who brings the question, challenge, data or clinical context (usually the proposer and their PI).
  • The contributing party: the people who join to deliver the work, for example AI, engineering or methods expertise (the collaborators).

Where a project has more than two groups, "both parties" means all parties named in the project's collaboration agreement.

2. Agree before you start (a priori agreement)

Every project and hackathon challenge must include a collaboration agreement before it can be submitted for review. It is shown on the project page, and anyone asking to join must accept it. At a minimum it should cover:

  • Background IP: what each party brings (datasets, code, models, methods) and confirmation that it remains theirs.
  • New IP (foreground): who owns what is created in the project (joint, one party, or as set out by King's policy) and how any commercial interest would be handled.
  • Software and model licence: e.g. open source (MIT, Apache-2.0), or private until the parties agree to release.
  • Authorship and credit: how authorship is decided (we recommend the CRediT taxonomy and ICMJE criteria), and who will be first and last author where this can be agreed early.
  • Data use: which data may be used, by whom, where it is stored, and what happens to it when the project ends.
  • Publication approval: how much time each party has to review an output before it is shared (we suggest 30 days).

If roles or scope change significantly (for example, a new partner joins), update the agreement and ask all parties to confirm the change.

3. Publications and outcomes need approval from both parties

  • No paper, preprint, abstract, poster, talk, blog post, code or model release, dataset, patent filing or press activity based on project work may be shared outside the project team without the written approval of both parties. Email is enough.
  • Send drafts to all parties in good time. Approval should not be unreasonably withheld or delayed. If you object, explain why and suggest changes, for example to protect patentable IP, confidential information or patient privacy.
  • Acknowledge every contributor fairly, as agreed, and acknowledge the FoLSM AI Collaboration Hub where appropriate.
  • Outcomes published on the hub's Impact page must also be approved by both parties.
  • These obligations continue after a project ends or a person leaves it.

4. Hackathon challenges and ideas

  • Whoever submits a challenge or idea keeps their background IP in it. Presenting an idea at a hackathon or on the hub does not give it away.
  • Each challenge states, in its collaboration agreement, the licence that hackathon code will be released under and how follow-on work would be agreed.
  • Results from hackathon work follow section 3: no external sharing without approval from both parties.

5. Data, confidentiality and governance

  • Never upload patient-identifiable or confidential data to this platform. The hub is for describing projects, not for storing data.
  • Use data only within its approvals (ethics, information governance, data-sharing agreements) and only for the agreed project.
  • Treat unpublished ideas, results and code shared with you as confidential to the project unless told otherwise.

6. Professional conduct

  • Be respectful, inclusive and constructive. Harassment or discrimination of any kind is not tolerated.
  • Keep your profile accurate, including your availability, and only commit time you can give.
  • Follow research-integrity standards: honest reporting, reproducibility and declaring conflicts of interest.
  • Use AI tools responsibly and transparently in your work, including when writing up.

7. Resolving disagreements

  1. Talk it through with the other party and the project PI.
  2. If that doesn't resolve it, contact the review panel coordinator, who can mediate.
  3. Matters about IP, contracts or misconduct are referred to the relevant King's College London teams (e.g. Research Contracts, IP & Licensing, Research Integrity).

8. Relationship to King's policies and contracts

This Code of Conduct sets expectations among hub members. It does not replace King's College London policies (including the IP policy and research-integrity policies), funder terms, or formal agreements such as collaboration agreements, data-sharing agreements or NDAs. Where these apply, they take precedence. Projects involving external partners, funding or commercial interest should put a formal agreement in place through King's Research Contracts.
